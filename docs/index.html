Scoring formula

opportunity = (D / C) × √P × M D demand growth vs the median niche = geomean(wiki_yoy ÷ median wiki_yoy, trends_yoy ÷ median trends_yoy) wiki_yoy = Wikipedia views, last 12 complete months ÷ previous 12 trends_yoy = geomean over keywords of (mean last 52 wks ÷ previous 52), each keyword queried alone; blank if the base year is mostly zeros C competition pressure vs the median niche (lower = less competition) = geomean(supply_growth ÷ median, median reach ÷ reach) supply_growth = uploads by each niche's ~20 leading channels, last 12 months ÷ previous 12, counted from their upload lists (missing = 1.0). YouTube search counts are not used: they cap at 1,000,000. reach = median views ÷ subscribers for recent videos from channels with ≥ 1,000 subscribers (≥ 20 channels per keyword) P demand size = percentile rank of mean z( log wiki views, log Trends level, log Reddit subs ) M money weight = niche mid RPM ÷ median mid RPM (1.0 while pending) D and C equal 1.0 for a typical niche.

Why this shape

Demand divided by competition is the core of keyword opportunity scores such as the Keyword Effectiveness Index. The target is the high-demand, low-supply quadrant from Parrot Analytics' demand vs supply matrix. Each signal is compared with the median niche first, because a raw growth number means little without a baseline (Google Flu Trends failed to beat a simple baseline: Bit by Bit). Reach, views per subscriber, is the "outlier" signal creators use: audience outrunning supply (OutlierKit). The √P term damps tiny niches: with no demand there is no opportunity, however low the competition.

Known limits

YouTube totalResults is an estimate, and YouTube stands in for still-image platforms that have no free count API. Wikipedia pageviews track search interest but weight broad topics heavily. Google Trends is read through pytrends, which is archived and often rate-limited. Reddit gives a single snapshot, so posting rate but not its growth.

Data: Wikimedia Pageviews API, Google Trends (pytrends), YouTube Data API v3, Reddit. Source code and raw CSVs: aseembhalla/niche-scout.
